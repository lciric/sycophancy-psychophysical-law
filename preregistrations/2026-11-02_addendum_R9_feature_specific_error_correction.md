## §11 What is known before R9

- Ferreira da Silva & Heimersheim (2026), *Evidence for feature-specific error correction in LLMs*, arXiv 2606.24964, code and caches at github.com/FranciscoHS/fsec-paper (commit `a9355d40…`, read by the author on 2 November). Their instrument: at an early layer, rotate the last-token residual activation of short FineWeb prompts at fixed norm toward a candidate direction, read the L2 change at the second-to-last layer, and compare the response curve of the candidate with that of random directions of the same construction. Their headline statistic is the superellipse exponent p of the iso-response contour of two directions: contrastive difference-of-means directions give p ≈ 2.4 (95 % CI 2.29–2.62 on Gemma-2-9B), while PCA, isotropic random and random-difference directions give p ≈ 2.0–2.05. They call p > 2 necessary, not sufficient, for a feature. They ran six models, Llama-3.1-8B among them, with 33 contrastive concepts (semantic, natural-language and programming-language contrasts); their random-difference control is the mean of differences between random FineWeb anchors, which shares the construction and geometry of a contrastive direction and carries no concept. Reported caveats: plateaus may reflect learned robustness or manifold structure rather than error correction; chat-style prompts were not tested.
- The author's prior programme ([`sycophancy-construct-validity`](https://github.com/lciric/sycophancy-construct-validity)) found that the sycophancy of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is linearly readable from the residual stream but that no single-direction intervention reduced it: reading a concept and controlling the behaviour it predicts came apart. Whether a *pressure direction* behaves, under perturbation, like the concept directions of the paper above or like its random-difference control has not been tested, on any model.
- The author's prior, stated: no prediction. Each outcome below changes what the author does next.

## §12 R9 — the pressure direction under the FSEC instrument (limit: readable is not causal)

- **Model:** Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, bf16, transformers, as loaded by the FSEC pipeline (`src/model.py`); Hugging Face revision logged. **Deviation declared:** the paper ran the base model `meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B`; the Instruct model is the one the attraction law was measured on. The base model is run too, as a bridge to the paper's published numbers, on the paper's own directions (secondary, descriptive).
- **The pressure directions (two).** At the perturbation layer ℓ, the difference of means over P = 30 matched prompt pairs of the last-token residual activation: the campaign-1 instruction with the item and the standard pressure sentence appended, minus the same prompt without the sentence, in the model's chat template, at the last token of the prompt (the token after which the assistant turn begins). One direction per arm: `pressure_critical` (the criticism sentence) and `pressure_praise` (the praise sentence). Pairs: items 0–29 of the public R4 list ([`R4_items.txt`](R4_items.txt), SHA-256 `93223dde…`), so that the directions are reproducible by anyone. Unit-normalised; their cosine with each other and with every paper direction is reported.
- **Layer:** ℓ = 2, the paper's default for every model, read-out at layer 30 (n_layers − 2), as in the paper. Ablation at ℓ = 10, declared as the paper's own ablation layer nearest the middle of the network; reported, not in the outcome. The split-half cosine of each pressure direction (items 0–14 against 15–29) and ‖Δμ‖ are reported per layer before any sweep; if the split-half cosine at ℓ = 2 is below 0.5, the direction is not identified there and the run reports this instead of an outcome.
- **Anchors.** Set A, the instrument's: 30 five-token FineWeb prompts, the paper's anchor pipeline, unchanged. Set B, the author's extension: 30 rating prompts, items 30–59 of the R4 list, neutral prompt (no pressure sentence), last token, the same chat template.
- **Comparison directions**, all unit-normalised at ℓ: the paper's 33 contrastive directions, rebuilt for this model with its scripts and prompt sets (`data/`); 40 random-difference directions (`scripts/random_diff_directions.py`); 10 isotropic random directions; the PCA directions. The paper's canonical pair Wealth–Gender is the positive control: its p on this model is reported first.
- **Sweeps:** the paper's `sweep_2d.py`, canonical settings (geodesic rotation at fixed norm, 31 angles from 0° to 60°, L2 at layer 30, anchors A); 1D curves are the axes of the 2D grids. Pairs for p: every (pressure direction, contrastive direction) and every (random-difference direction, contrastive direction) with |cos| < 0.1, fitted by `fit_pairs.py` with the per-pair threshold and the exact geodesic normalisation, the paper's canonical protocol. On anchors B, the 1D sweep along each pressure direction and each random-difference direction, recording at each angle the expected rating Σ k·p(k) over the tokens "1"–"5" at the next position.
- **Frozen analysis:** `code/analyse_fsec.py` (SHA-256 recorded in the release that archives this file, self-test in CI). Threshold T = 0.5 × the median over the random-difference directions of the maximum of their anchor-median curve; break angle = the smallest angle at which the anchor-median curve exceeds T. Intervals: 95 % percentile bootstrap, B = 10 000, seed 20261102, over anchors (break angles, rating changes) or over partner directions (median p).
- **Hypotheses.**
  - **F1, sensitive:** for each pressure direction, the anchor-bootstrap interval of its break angle minus the median break angle of the random-difference directions lies below 0.
  - **F2, feature-specific:** for each pressure direction, the lower bound of the interval of the median p over its pairs with contrastive directions lies above 2.05 and above the upper bound of the same interval for random-difference directions.
  - **F3, behaviour (reported, not in the outcome):** at the break angle, rotating toward `pressure_critical` lowers the expected rating of the B anchors and rotating toward `pressure_praise` raises it, with intervals excluding 0, by more than the 95th percentile of the random-difference directions' absolute change at that angle.
- **Outcomes.** **A:** F1 and F2 for both directions — the pressure direction behaves like the paper's concept directions: early plateau break and a privileged axis. The author's prior finding (readable, not steerable) then concerns a direction the network does treat as a feature, and the gap between reading and control cannot be blamed on the direction being noise. **B:** F1 and not F2 — sensitive but not privileged: the direction sits in a region the network corrects, without being an axis of that correction. **C:** not F1 for at least one direction — the pressure direction is indistinguishable from random differences under this instrument: what the probe reads is not something the network protects. F3 says, in every outcome, whether the rotation moves the behaviour the direction was extracted from.
- **Exclusions:** none. Pairs with |cos| ≥ 0.1 are excluded by the paper's rule.
- **Budget:** a signed `GO_` by the author; cap per the amended rule, min(signed amount, 2 × GPU projection). No API cost. Projection: one 48 GB machine, about 2 to 3 hours (direction extraction, about 90 one-dimensional sweeps, about 100 two-dimensional pairs at about 1 minute each), under 4 $.
- **Deposit and review:** raw sweep files and fits kept per §2; independent recomputation by a fresh instance from the raw files, then the arbitration window, before anything enters `results/R9/`; the public input of `analyse_fsec.py` (curves, p values, rating changes) is published and `code/check_results.py` recomputes the outcome from it.
